Bring It Back – Privacy Policy

Last updated: 18 November 2026

Overview

Bring It Back ("the App") is developed by DevCodeApps. The App helps you find and restore photos, videos, audio and documents that your phone still keeps (in the phone's trash, in a gallery's recycle bin, in hidden or moved folders, and in cached copies), and it keeps a recycle bin of files you delete with the App. This Privacy Policy explains what the App does with your information.

In short: scanning, previews, restores and the recycle bin all happen on your phone. The App never uploads your photos, videos, files, file names or folder contents to us or to anyone else. There is no account. The free plan shows full-screen ads from Google AdMob, so the App has internet access for ads and for the consent choice that goes with them, and Google's ads software collects the data described under "Internet Access and Advertising" below. The Pro subscription shows no ads. The App also uses Google's Firebase services, Google Analytics for Firebase and Firebase Crashlytics, to count which features are used (by screen name and in coarse ranges only) and to receive crash reports, so we can improve the App and fix crashes. Where the law requires your consent (for example in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom), none of this is collected until you agree in Google's consent form; where it does not, it runs from the first start. See "Analytics and Crash Reports (Firebase)" below.

What the App Accesses on Your Device

The App works with the following information. It uses it only to provide the features you ask for. None of the items below is sent to us or to anyone else.

  • Photos, videos and audio (media access): With your permission, the App reads the media library on your phone, including items in Android's trash (Android 11 and later). It uses this to list what can be restored, to show previews and thumbnails, to copy files you choose to restore, and to find files that are hidden or in unusual folders. On Android 14 and later you can allow only selected photos; in that case Android hides the trash from the App and the App asks you to allow all photos and videos if you want the trash scanned.
  • Folders you choose: If you connect a folder (for example a gallery's recycle bin or a messenger's media folder) with Android's folder picker, the App can read the files in that folder and keeps that access until you remove it. It cannot read other folders. Folders you choose can contain documents as well as media.
  • File details: The App reads file names, folders, sizes, dates and types, and the photo or video's own metadata such as its date and, if the file has it, its location. The App does not use location data for anything: it is only copied unchanged along with the file when you restore it. The App does not read your device's location.
  • Installed apps (limited): The App checks only whether a short, fixed list of gallery and file-manager apps is installed (such as Google Photos and Samsung Gallery), so that it can show you the right guide and open that app. It does not read your list of installed apps.
  • Files you restore: Restored files are copied to a folder on your phone (for example Pictures/Recovered) or to a folder you choose. The original in the trash, bin or hidden folder is not changed.
  • Recycle bin ("Delete safely"): When you delete photos or videos with the App's Delete safely feature, the App keeps an exact copy in a private recycle bin inside the App's own storage, and Android asks you to confirm the deletion of the original. The bin keeps copies for a limited time (7 days and up to 500 MB on the free plan, 90 days and up to 5 GB with Pro) and then removes them. The bin holds only files you deleted through the App; it does not capture files deleted in other apps.
  • Scan results and settings: The list of items found by a scan, the recycle bin's records, your settings, the number of free restores used today and the last confirmed Pro status are stored in the App's private storage on your phone. Android's app backup is turned off for the App, so this data is not included in Android backups.

Internet Access and Advertising (Free Plan)

The App has the INTERNET permission for these reasons: to show ads, to ask for your consent choice, and to send the usage statistics and crash reports described under "Analytics and Crash Reports (Firebase)" below. Scanning, previews, restores and the recycle bin do not use the internet. The App never sends your photos, videos, files, file names or folder contents to Google's advertising service, to Google's Firebase services or to anyone else.

When ads appear

  • Only on the free plan. The App shows full-screen (interstitial) ads only; there are no banners and no ads inside scans, previews or restores.
  • Only right after you close a restore result: when you close the restore summary on the Results screen, or when you leave Preview after restoring a file there.
  • Never in the first session after you install the App. In that session the ads software is not started and no consent form is shown.
  • At most one ad per minute, and never right after the App shows Google Play's review card.
  • Pro has no ads. When the App starts on a phone with Pro, it does not start Google's ads software, does not show the consent form and does not request ads, so the ads data collection described under "Who provides the ads" below does not take place. This applies to the ads software only: usage statistics and crash reports (see "Analytics and Crash Reports (Firebase)" below) are not ads and can still run on Pro. If you subscribe while the App is open, ads stop at once; the ads software stops being started from the next time you open the App. If a subscription ends, the free plan's ads (and the data collection that comes with them) start again from the next time you open the App.

Who provides the ads: Google AdMob

Ads are provided by Google AdMob, a third party, through Google's Mobile Ads SDK built into the App. According to Google's published data disclosure for that software, it automatically collects and shares the following data for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention:

  • IP address. It may be used to estimate the general location of your device.
  • Product interactions. Interaction information such as app launch, taps and video views.
  • Diagnostic information. Information about how the App and the ads software perform, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy usage.
  • Device and account identifiers. The Android advertising ID and the app set ID and, if applicable, other identifiers related to signed-in accounts on the phone.

Google uses this data to show ads, to measure how ads perform, and to prevent fraud. Google says all of the data the ads software collects is encrypted in transit with TLS. Google handles it under Google's Privacy Policy; to learn how Google uses information from apps that use its services, see How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.

Your choices

  • Consent form. Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom), the App shows Google's consent form, built with Google's User Messaging Platform. No ad is requested before your consent choice allows it. The same choice also decides whether usage statistics and crash reports are collected (see "Analytics and Crash Reports (Firebase)" below). Where this applies, Privacy options on the Pro screen reopens the form so you can change your choice at any time.
  • Advertising ID. You can reset or delete the Android advertising ID in your phone's Android settings (ad ID controls).
  • Pro. Subscribing to Pro ends the ads.

Analytics and Crash Reports (Firebase)

The App uses two services from Google's Firebase platform: Google Analytics for Firebase (usage statistics) and Firebase Crashlytics (crash reports). We use them to learn which parts of the App are used and where it needs work, and to find and fix crashes, so that we can improve the App. We do not use them to show you ads. We run no server of our own to receive this data; Google processes it for us, and we see it in the Firebase console. There is no account, and none of this data contains your name or email address. None of it contains your photos, videos, files, file names, folder paths or folder contents.

What the App sends to Google Analytics

The App sends only fixed names and coarse ranges (for example "21 to 100 items" or "over 100 items"; a count of none or one is sent as it is):

  • Screens opened, by screen name only (for example Home, Results, Preview, Pro).
  • Setup and scans: when the first-run setup is finished; when a scan finishes, how many items it found per source (phone trash, gallery bin, chosen folders, lost media) as ranges, whether you stopped it, and how long it took as a range.
  • Restores: when a restore finishes, how many items were attempted and restored as ranges, whether any failed (yes or no), whether it was done from the Results or the Preview screen, and whether the files went to the default folders or to a folder you chose.
  • Recycle bin: when a Delete safely or share-to-bin action finishes, whether it was started with Delete safely or from the share sheet, and how many items were kept or not deleted as ranges.
  • Free plan and Pro: when the free daily limit is reached, when the Pro screen is shown, when a purchase is started and how it ended (and which plan kind), when a full-screen ad is shown, and when the App asks Google Play to show its review card.
  • Whether you have Pro (yes or no), as a property attached to the statistics.

The App never sends a file name, a folder path, a file's content or an error message to Google Analytics.

Google's Analytics software also collects some data automatically. According to Google's published data disclosure for it, this includes: an app instance ID (an ID Google generates for each installation of the App, used to count unique installations and compute user metrics); the Android advertising ID (unless you have opted out of ad personalization in your phone's Android settings); coarse location derived from a masked IP address; screen views and sessions; purchase and subscription events with details such as product ID and price; and information about your device, such as its model and Android version.

What the App sends to Firebase Crashlytics

If the App crashes, a crash report is sent to Google. According to Google, Crashlytics collects the stack trace (the code location of the crash), relevant application state and information about the device at the time (such as its model, Android version, processor type, memory and storage size, and whether it is rooted), the time of the crash, the App's version, session information, and an installation ID that lets us count how many people a crash affects. Because Analytics is also on, a crash report can include recent Analytics events as "breadcrumb" logs. The App adds only two items of its own to a crash report: the name of the screen that was open and whether you have Pro. We do not add file names, folder paths or file contents to crash reports.

App settings (Firebase Remote Config)

The App downloads a small set of settings from Google's Firebase Remote Config, such as whether ads may appear and how often, how often the App may ask you to rate it, and the oldest version of the App that may still be used. According to Google, the request includes an identifier for this installation of the App (a Firebase installation ID, which does not identify you or your phone), your phone's country code, language code, time zone and Android version, the App's package name and Firebase app ID, and the version of Google's Remote Config software. It contains no file names and nothing about your files. The App needs these settings to work as intended, so the download is not tied to the consent choice below; it happens at most about every 12 hours. If the settings cannot be downloaded, the App uses its built-in values.

Your choices: consent

  • Where the law requires your consent (for example in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, as decided by Google's consent check), the App collects no usage statistics and no crash reports until you agree in Google's consent form. The form is shown where the App is about to use ads (on the free plan, from the second time you open the App), never in the first session, so in these regions nothing is collected in the first session. If you do not agree, nothing is collected. The Analytics consent settings for analytics storage, ad storage, ad user data and ad personalization follow the choices you make in that form.
  • Where the law does not require consent, usage statistics and crash reports run from the first time you open the App. There is no switch in the App to turn them off there.
  • One choice covers ads, usage statistics and crash reports. Where the form applies, Privacy options on the Pro screen reopens it, so you can change or withdraw your choice at any time.
  • Pro: a Pro phone never shows the consent form for ads. Where consent is required, nothing is therefore collected on a Pro phone unless you agree through Privacy options on the Pro screen. Where consent is not required, usage statistics and crash reports also run on a Pro phone, because Pro removes ads, not these services.
  • Advertising ID. You can reset or delete the Android advertising ID, or opt out of ad personalization, in your phone's Android settings.

How long Google keeps this data, and who sees it

  • Crash reports: Google says Crashlytics keeps crash stack traces, extracted minidump data and associated identifiers for 90 days before starting the process of removing them from its live and backup systems.
  • Usage statistics: Google Analytics keeps event data for a retention period set in the Analytics settings of the App's Firebase project: 2 months or 14 months for a standard property, according to Google.
  • Who receives it: the data goes to Google. Google says Firebase does not transfer this data to third parties except to subprocessors that help provide the services and as we instruct, and that it encrypts the data in transit using HTTPS (and TLS for Google Analytics). The App's Google AdMob account is linked to the same Firebase project, so AdMob can show us user metrics based on the Analytics data.
  • More information: Privacy and Security in Firebase and Google's Privacy Policy. Google describes the data the Firebase software collects on Firebase's Google Play data disclosure page.

What We Do Not Collect

  • We do not upload, receive or store your files, photos, videos, audio or documents, or their names, folders or previews.
  • We do not collect your name, email address, phone number or contacts. No account is needed to use the App.
  • The App does not ask for your device's location.
  • We run no server that receives data from the App. The only data that leaves your phone through the App is the ad and consent data, the usage statistics and crash reports, and the settings download described above, which go to Google, plus the purchase check with Google Play described below. None of it contains your files, their names or your folder contents.

  • Google AdMob and Google's User Messaging Platform (free plan): Show ads and manage your consent choice, as described under "Internet Access and Advertising" above. Google is a separate company with its own privacy policy: Google's Privacy Policy.
  • Google Analytics for Firebase, Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Remote Config (Google Firebase): Provide usage statistics and crash reports, subject to your consent choice where consent is required, and the App's settings download, as described under "Analytics and Crash Reports (Firebase)" above. Google handles purchase data under Google's Privacy Policy and the Google Play terms.

The App contains no other third-party services that receive data.

Permissions Explained

  • READ_MEDIA_IMAGES, READ_MEDIA_VIDEO, READ_MEDIA_AUDIO (Android 13 and later) and READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE (Android 12 and earlier): Let the App see the photos, videos and audio on your phone, including the system trash, so they can be listed, previewed and restored, and so the Delete safely feature can copy a file before it is deleted.
  • READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED (Android 14 and later): Lets the App tell "Allow limited access" (selected photos only) apart from full access, so it can explain that the trash is hidden in that case.
  • ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION: Without it, Android removes the location data from photos and videos the App reads, and the copies it makes would not be exact. The App uses it only to keep that data in the copy. It does not use or send your location.
  • INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE: Let the ads software, the consent form and Google's Firebase software connect to Google and check whether the phone is online. The App uses INTERNET for ads, consent, usage statistics and crash reports only.
  • com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION and ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS: Added by Google's ads software; Google's Analytics software also declares AD_ID, and Google says Analytics collects the advertising ID by default. They let the software use the advertising ID and Android's ad services (ad measurement and ad topics) for ads and measurement.
  • com.google.android.finsky.permission.BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE: Added by Google's Analytics software. It lets Google Analytics learn which Google Play listing or campaign led to the install.
  • com.android.vending.BILLING: Lets the App talk to Google Play to show prices and check your Pro subscription.
  • RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED and WAKE_LOCK: Used by Android's WorkManager library, which the App uses to remove expired recycle-bin items once a day and to retry confirming a purchase with Google Play. RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED lets WorkManager schedule that work again after the phone restarts. WAKE_LOCK is also declared by Google's Analytics software. The App does not use them to collect data.
  • DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION (the App's own): A standard Android protection that keeps the App's internal messages private to the App.

The App does not use "All files access" and does not run a foreground service.

Data Security

Your files stay on your phone and are protected by your phone's own security (screen lock and encryption). The recycle bin sits in the App's private storage, which other apps cannot read. Files you restore are saved in normal folders on your phone, where other apps with media access can see them, like any other photo or video. The ad and consent data, usage statistics and crash reports described above are sent to Google; Google says the ads software encrypts its data in transit with TLS and that Firebase encrypts its data in transit using HTTPS.

Data Retention and Deletion

We run no server of our own and hold no account data about you, so there is nothing for us to delete on a server of ours. The only data we can see about the App's use is the usage statistics and crash reports in our Google Firebase project, described below. On your phone you are in control:

  • Empty the recycle bin or delete single items in the App's Protect tab at any time. Items are also removed automatically when their time is up.
  • Remove a connected folder in the App to end its access.
  • Uninstalling the App or clearing its data in Android settings deletes the scan results, settings and everything in the recycle bin.
  • Files you restored are normal files in your own folders (for example Pictures/Recovered). They stay until you delete them.
  • Data that Google's ads software collected is held by Google, not by us. You can reset or delete the advertising ID in Android settings, and you can use Google's own privacy tools through Google's Privacy Policy.
  • Usage statistics and crash reports are held by Google for the periods given under "Analytics and Crash Reports (Firebase)" above, and then removed. Where the law requires your consent, you can stop further collection by withdrawing it under Privacy options on the Pro screen. Google says Firebase enables developers to delete end-user data; because the App has no account, we cannot link a deletion request to you by name. Uninstalling the App does not by itself remove data Google already holds.
  • Purchase records are held by Google. Manage or cancel your subscription in Google Play (Subscriptions), and contact Google about purchase data.

Children's Privacy

The App is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children, and the App has no account.
